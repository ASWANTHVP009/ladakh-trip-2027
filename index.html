Important reality check — this is an aggressive schedule. It includes several 400–600 km highway days, limited weather buffer, and a high-altitude Hanle/Umling La loop. It is not a relaxed 7-hour-per-day itinerary. If you want to cap riding at about 300 km / 7 hours, extend the trip by at least 5–7 days or drop Agra/Delhi and the Hanle–Umling La extension. Do not try to recover lost time by riding tired or after dark.
Route logic: I used the PDF’s core sequence (Agra, Delhi, Amritsar, Srinagar, Kargil, Leh, Nubra, Turtuk, Pangong, Hanle, Umling La, Manali) and the screenshot’s named stops where they fit. Jaipur, Ajmer and Lonavala are not on the direct route to Chandigarh and would create major detours; they are excluded. Golden Temple is included at Amritsar. The return ends at Chandigarh—not Kerala—because the bike is to be parcelled and you travel home by train.

DAY 21 · 18 JUN

Manali — full sightseeing day (Atal Tunnel & Sissu excursion)

~100–140 km local return ride · 4–6 hrs riding

Route: Manali → Hadimba Temple → Manu Temple / Old Manali → Solang Valley → Atal Tunnel → Sissu → return to Manali via Atal Tunnel → Vashisht → Mall Road. Sissu is conditional on the road beyond the tunnel being open and safe.

VISITING PLACES — POINT BY POINT
  1. Hadimba Devi Temple — Morning visit in the cedar forest; allow time for parking and walking.
  2. Manu Temple — Visit in Old Manali; access involves a short walk.
  3. Old Manali — Explore the lanes and have breakfast / coffee.
  4. Solang Valley — Mountain views and optional activities; check operator safety and weather.
  5. Atal Tunnel — Ride through only; do not stop inside the tunnel. Follow traffic and portal restrictions.
  6. Sissu Lake and waterfall — Visit only if the road beyond the tunnel is open and weather is safe.
  7. Vashisht Temple and hot springs — Optional late-afternoon visit; check opening and bathing arrangements.
  8. Manali Mall Road — Evening stroll and dinner.
  • Start early (around 7:00 AM). This is a full sightseeing day, so keep stops short and do not add extra activities that make the return after dark.
  • Atal Tunnel is a transit tunnel: never stop inside it. Obey restrictions at both portals and do not park on the carriageway.
  • If weather, traffic or road status makes the Sissu excursion unsuitable, spend the day around Manali / Solang instead.
  • Overnight: Manali. Keep the bike packed and ready for an early departure to Chandigarh on 19 June.

Permits, road status and essential checks

  • Ladakh Environment / tourist fee: Use the official Leh District Tourist Management System and confirm the current domestic tourist registration / fee requirements, circuits and check-post documents. Carry printed and offline digital copies. Official Leh permit portal.
  • Route-specific access: Nubra, Turtuk, Pangong, Hanle and Umling La are subject to permitted circuits, check-post verification, security directions and current restrictions. The official portal lists circuits; verify the exact circuit for your dates. Official permitted circuits.
  • 48-hour acclimatisation: Leh district says visitors must acclimatise for at least 48 hours before higher-altitude destinations. District advisory.
  • Manali–Leh highway: Seasonal opening is variable. The official district road-status page is useful for Lahaul-side updates, but also check BRO / Himachal Police / Ladakh Police and local administration immediately before departure. Lahaul-Spiti road status.
  • Srinagar–Leh / Zoji La: Check J&K traffic police and local convoy timings. A road shown as open can still have controlled one-way windows.
  • Bike papers: RC, valid insurance, PUC, driving licence, ID, emergency contacts, and any lender NOC / authorisation if applicable. Confirm insurance covers high-altitude travel and pillion.
  • International / border sensitivity: Do not photograph military installations, check posts, bridges or restricted areas. Follow all Army / ITBP / police instructions.

Health, altitude and medicines

Medical note: This is a packing guide, not a prescription. Speak to a travel-medicine doctor before the trip—especially before using acetazolamide (Diamox), antibiotics, anti-vomiting medicines or painkillers. Do not self-medicate to push through altitude symptoms.
  • AMS warning signs: headache, nausea, dizziness, unusual tiredness, loss of appetite or disturbed sleep. Stop ascending and seek medical advice if symptoms appear.
  • Emergency signs: breathlessness at rest, worsening cough, confusion, poor coordination, repeated vomiting, severe headache or altered consciousness. Descend urgently with assistance and seek emergency care. Do not leave the affected rider alone.
  • Acclimatisation: at least 48 hours in Leh before higher areas; no hard exercise on arrival; avoid alcohol, smoking and sedatives; hydrate regularly and eat light. Follow current local medical advice rather than a fixed water target if you have a medical restriction.
  • Medicines to discuss with your doctor: personal prescription medicines; acetazolamide only if clinically suitable and prescribed; paracetamol; ORS; prescribed anti-nausea medicine; basic antiseptic / wound dressing supplies; motion-sickness medicine if needed; allergy medicine if appropriate; sunscreen and lip balm. Carry prescriptions and original packaging.
  • Do not rely on portable oxygen cans as treatment. They are not a substitute for descent and medical care. A pulse oximeter can be a supplementary observation tool, not a clearance to keep climbing.
  • Medical access: Facilities are limited outside Leh. Save the Ladakh emergency contacts and local hospital details offline. Emergency number 112; SNM Hospital Leh tele-consultation desk: 01982-256907 (verify before travel).

Couple’s riding precautions

  • Agree on a hand signal for stop, fatigue, nausea, cold and mechanical issues. Stop every 60–90 minutes and swap / stretch where safe.
  • Keep pillion luggage light and balanced; heavy items low and close to the centre. Do not overload the G 310 GS beyond the manufacturer’s payload limit—check the owner’s manual and include both people, luggage and accessories.
  • Do not ride at night in the hills. Start early, plan fuel and food stops, and reach accommodation before dark.
  • Never ride through a flooded stream or fast water crossing. Wait for local advice; do not follow another vehicle blindly.
  • Keep one day’s essential medicines, warm layer, water, snacks and documents accessible—not buried in luggage.
  • Share the daily route and expected arrival time with a family member. Check in at each overnight stop.
  • Use a strict turn-back rule: weather worsening, road closure, symptoms, low fuel, fading daylight or fatigue means stop / turn back.

Bike parcel from Chandigarh — before you leave

  1. Contact Chandigarh railway parcel office in advance and confirm that they accept a motorcycle parcel to your chosen Kerala station, the current tariff, packing requirements, train availability and expected transit time.
  2. Ask whether the bike must travel as a parcel (separately) or can be booked as luggage on the same train you travel on. These are different procedures; availability is not guaranteed.
  3. Carry original RC, government ID, train ticket if booking as luggage, and any forms / forwarding note required by the parcel office. Confirm whether an authorised representative can collect it at destination.
  4. Before packing: wash and dry the bike; reduce fuel to the level directed by the parcel office (often near-empty); disconnect battery only if instructed; remove / secure mirrors and loose accessories; protect the windscreen and bodywork.
  5. Photograph the bike from all sides, odometer, fuel level, accessories and existing marks before handover. Keep valuables, helmet, riding gear, tools and documents with you—not inside the parcel.
  6. Check the consignment receipt, destination station, sender / receiver details and contact number. Keep a photo and physical copy until delivery.
  7. Do not assume same-day parcel acceptance or delivery. Indian Railways’ published guidance says parcel handling and luggage rules differ; contact the station parcel office for the current process. Two-wheeler booking guidance · Railway luggage rules.
